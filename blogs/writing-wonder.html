Feel Wonder While You Wander

"Concrete Jungle" by FHG Photo

Mother Nature is begging her rebellious children to behave.

Even though the city's population is decreasing, in Latvia's capital Riga, we leave empty concrete jungles instead of real parks and gardens. The privatization of public goods is so prevalent in this society, that there is no space to appreciate our Mother's diligent work. What an awful case of unrequited love!

All my life I have lived next to Biķernieki forest – a beautiful place where you can find solace and tranquillity after stressful days of work. Every morning I looked out the window opening out to a small field with berry bushes and flowers. I remember my sister screaming one morning and when I ran to her thinking that there was an intruder or that she got stabbed, she exclaimed "Look!" and pointed at the window. In the field we saw a small family of red foxes – two cubs and a very apprehensive mom – looking for a rat to catch. It was beautiful. Now, however, my window overlooks three seven-storey luxury apartment buildings that have not been lived in for ages – ever since they were built.

We went from a forest to an apartment complex.

The thing is that Purvciems is not the only place that has been subjected to these sorts of changes. With every year, Riga's number of micro-districts is rapidly increasing, with new projects like Skanste and Dreiliņi appearing out of nowhere in a blink of an eye.

According to city24.lv, the number of offers in Riga micro-districts last year was 66% higher in September than at the beginning of the year and these projects are mostly luxury complexes, with the average price of 2 046 EUR/m2. So affordable! Especially for citizens of a country that has a 500-euro minimum wage.

These sudden changes force Riga's inhabitants to feel closed off to the natural world, making them focus more and more on materialistic things. You see, when everything around you has a price (even the weeds growing through the cracks of the pavement on your way to school) the only thing on your mind is money. In this capitalistic world, the green grass has been replaced by green bills.

"Capitalism is still winning around the world, albeit slowly," says Robert A. Lawson, Chair in Economic Freedom at the SMU Cox School of Business. Indeed, it does. Nowadays, the price is being often confused with the value. Value has gone from being a category at the core of economic theory to a subjective category tied to the 'preferences' of economic agents. As nature is free for everyone, it doesn't have that much value in the eyes of the greedy businessmen unless it has been privatized for profit.

This raises a question: are we really doing all of this for our own benefit?

For me, someone who walks 10 000 steps on a daily basis, hikes are extremely important. Every time I go outside, however, I am faced with the grey facades of depressing uninhabited buildings. One in particular has been empty for ages, it was built even before I was born and has now become a great place for kids to do drugs after school.

New micro-districts and new apartment complexes take away the opportunity to appreciate nature in its true form. In order to relax after gruelling hours of work, you need to heal and Mother Nature's offerings are the best remedies. And also free of cost.

Our future is in the hands of the richest people, so the only thing that you can do is enjoy it as much as you can – looking up the big blue sky, exploring the variety of tall trees and walking on the green grass. In this constantly changing world, you have to appreciate every second of the natural beauty around you, you never know what might happen tomorrow.

Who knows, there might be a bunch of McMansions next time you visit your favourite forest.
