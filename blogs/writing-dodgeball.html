Dodgeball

Vivek Nikam / Triangle News

The bell rang and just as people were starting to rush out of their classrooms I moved closer to the timber double doors that lead to the school gym. The woodwork of the left door caught my eye. It looked a tad bit crooked compared to the right one. I suppose that no wood can withhold the amount of aggressive ball hits that the doors took on a daily basis.

My eyes were following the elliptical curve of the wood grooves and in a fraction of a second I wondered why did the groove start to appear bigger. The deafening screams of some losing team pulled me back into reality and I jumped backwards at least two feet. That wouldn't be the last time that my nose was on the brink of breaking and that was only today.

The pungent stench of prepubescent boys' perspiration hit one of my nostril, while the suffocating odour of a cheap deodorant overtook the other. The scent is probably called "Winter War", "Pine Punch" or something like that.

My PE teacher yelled: "Today we have dodgeball" and joyous expressions of my classmates baffled me. What's there to be happy about? In less than ten minutes' time this place will become a war zone.

I put on my sports clothes grudgingly and waited for what felt like inevitable execution. One, two, one, two. I'm in the second team which saddened me a bit, because it was further from the door and the odds of escaping unharmed started to decrease by the second.

The looks of complete concentration and ardor of the rival team frightened me so much that I thought about making a run for it. The flashbacks of my previous injuries took over my senses that I was almost hit in the abdomen. Scary, truly scary. The heavy breathing filled the air and I found myself jumping from one side of the gym to another like a rabbit trying to confuse a predator.

This struggle reminded me a lot of the wildlife. In fact, it was, just like in real life.

I stayed away from the action, always trying to avoid the ball instead of seizing it once and for all and throwing it as hard as I possibly could. I was just always too scared to cause anyone harm, so I stood on the sidelines, observing, trying to scrape through without anyone noticing and aiming the ball at me.

Suddenly the ball softly rolled to my feet. At such a small distance it looked harmless. Battered by thousands of collisions with the cement walls, it has lost its original shape and had a small, but noticeable dent. And just as I was thinking of a metaphorical way to describe the longevity of one's life, my teammates' roars pierced my ears.

I took every once of willpower and courage that I had in myself, grabbed the ball with my right hand and threw it right at my most cocky classmate. Bullseye. More roars.

I felt a sudden power surge in my arms and legs. I felt excited, happy, I wanted more. Is that how others feel when they finally take their life into their own hands? The next 20 minutes I spent trying to seize the ball at every possibility while still standing my ground. I no longer leapt and danced around in fear of getting hit. I grabbed the ball, aimed it, threw it and repeated the process. The opposite team started to fall one by one while desperately jumping around. The taste of power was something I've never felt before, it was sweet, but salty, gave you everything you wanted, but left you hungry, wanting more. I hit the ball too hard this time that it collided with the timber doors and the doors flung open. I saw two big eyes of a petrified girl gawping at me and the face disappeared at the sound of the school bell. I won, I finally won.
