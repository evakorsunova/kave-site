A Life Path Is A Maze

"Verirrt I (Lost in the woods)" by Mihaly Munkacsy

The present times have opened a door to thousands of new possibilities. The days of inheriting your family's profession are long behind us. You can become a preacher even if your dad owned a brothel, you just have to pay God (some MLM Christian organization) for your and your family's sins (a month-long course). Hooray!

However, as you grow older, you realize that the abundance of career choices can be overwhelming and the thing that blinds you before you even get to the more blinding (supposedly) "bright future" is the need to decide on who to be in this lifetime.

It's the same overwhelming feeling you get trying to find something to watch on Netflix while eating. The second "tudum" leaves the screen, you are bombarded with flashy posters of hundreds of shows and movies. How can someone decide on what to watch from all of this? The dollar store noodles are too special for shitty entertainment – you have to pick carefully. And while you search and search for the perfect show, the noodles have already turned colder than all of the films about the Cold War.

During moments like these, I remember the passage from Sylvia Plath's The Bell Jar:

I saw my life branching out before me like the green fig tree in the story. From the tip of every branch, like a fat purple fig, a wonderful future beckoned and winked... I saw myself sitting in the crotch of this fig tree, starving to death, just because I couldn't make up my mind which of the figs I would choose. I wanted each and every one of them, but choosing one meant losing all the rest, and, as I sat there, unable to decide, the figs began to wrinkle and go black, and, one by one, they plopped to the ground at my feet.

The metaphor of the fig tree speaks to the universal human experience of grappling with the desire to explore every avenue while acknowledging the impossibility of such a thing. It's also the reason why this book is one of my all-time favorites.

So, how can one decide? I should not be complaining, because having a choice is always a privilege, but the variety of choices stops me from making one. Because why in hell should I settle? Why should I have to choose only one if there are infinite possibilities?

This is why after a long search for definite meaning in this world, I have realized that there isn't one.

A man does not have only one quality and one talent. He cannot be put into a box because it doesn't even exist. It's just an illusion that we have forced ourselves to believe in as a way to cope with life's inherent ambiguity. Every one of us has multiple things that we're good at, defying confinement to a single identity, so why should we focus only on one?

I'm convinced that my future will not be tied to one particular occupation and I will float like a plastic bag in the wind from one field's doorstep to another. That is exactly why, ever since childhood, I have been growing my skill set into oblivion. I know that it is not the best choice as employers favor those with a more in-depth skill set, but I want to explore myself as much as possible. That's what life is all about!!!

Additionally, I can fit almost any job description. It's like building a safety net of abilities, preparing for any potential scenario, no matter how unlikely it may seem.

What if I get stranded on an island and, in order for the native tribe to take me under their wing, I need to woo them with my macrame skills?! Or! (with much greater possibility) – what if during my job interview, the interviewer suddenly realizes that they forgot to book a belly dancer for their office party that just happens to be on the same day?! I will surely save the day and get the job! What a win-win situation!

Why do you think I am even writing this article right now? It's for me to put "shitty journalist" as my hundredth possible career option right between the "deceitful Depop reseller" and "mediocre masseuse".
